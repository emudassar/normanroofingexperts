## The far end of the county

Lexington sits at the southern edge of Cleveland County on the Canadian River, across from
Purcell. It's a genuinely separate town rather than a Norman suburb, with its own history and
a main street that predates most of what's north of it.

For roofing, the two facts that matter are distance and age. Distance determines who's willing
to come. Age determines what they'll find when they get here.

## Old houses have complicated roofs, even when they look simple

A modest single-storey house in Lexington can be a more involved job than a much larger modern
home, and the reasons aren't visible from the street:

**Layer count.** Roofs laid over previous roofs, sometimes more than once, over several
decades of ownership. Every extra layer is more tear-off labour and more disposal weight, and
it hides whatever the decking has been doing underneath.

**Plank decking.** Individual boards, often with gaps, rather than sheet material. Fastening
is different, holding power varies board to board, and a century of accumulated nail holes
affects both. Sometimes overlay sheathing is the right answer; sometimes it isn't.

**Previous repairs of mixed quality.** Sealant over failed flashing. A patch of mismatched
shingles. Roofing cement where a proper detail was needed. None of it fatal, all of it
relevant to what a new roof needs to correct.

**Structures framed on site.** Older roofs were cut and framed by carpenters rather than
delivered as engineered trusses. Rafter spacing and sizing vary, and that occasionally
matters if a heavier covering is being considered.

None of this makes a Lexington roof difficult. It makes it a job that has to be assessed
before it's priced, which is why we don't quote roofs over the phone.

## Getting a fair deal at the edge of the market

Being further out reduces competition, and reduced competition affects price. The practical
defence is straightforward: get more than one written quote, and compare them on scope rather
than on the number at the bottom.

Ask every contractor, in writing:

- Is this a full tear-off with disposal included, or a layover?
- How many layers do you expect to remove?
- What is your per-sheet rate for replacing bad decking, and what triggers replacement?
- Is all flashing being replaced, or reused?
- What underlayment, and where does ice-and-water membrane go?
- What does your workmanship warranty cover, and will you be in business to honour it?

Two quotes thousands apart nearly always differ on those answers, not on the shingle.

## Maintenance is worth more on an older roof

Where slopes are shaded and slow to dry, common on properties nearer the river and under
mature trees, the highest-value maintenance is unglamorous: keep valleys and gutters clear
through autumn, trim back anything overhanging the roof, and deal with moss before it holds
moisture against the shingles through a whole winter.
