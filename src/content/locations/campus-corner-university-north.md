## What roofing near the OU campus actually involves

The area immediately north of the university is one of the most distinctive roofing
environments in Norman, and not because of the architecture. It's because of ownership
patterns.

Housing here turns over constantly. Properties are bought as investments, held, patched,
and sold on. Each transaction tends to reset the maintenance record to zero, because the new owner
knows what the inspection report said and little else. Roofs accumulate quick fixes:
sealant over a flashing that needed replacing, a patch of mismatched shingles over a
persistent leak, a layer laid over an old roof to avoid the cost of tear-off.

None of that is unusual and none of it is a crisis. It just means the honest starting point
for most roofs near campus is an inspection that establishes what's actually up there,
rather than a quote based on what the roof looks like from the street.

## The details we check first here

**Transitions between original roof and additions.** Nearly every older house in this area
has been added to. Where a lower, shallower roof meets an original steeper one, water
concentrates and the flashing detail carries all the load.

**Low-slope sections that were shingled.** Asphalt shingles need slope to work. Enclosed
porches and flat-roofed rear extensions get shingled anyway because it's quicker and the
match looks better. They leak, usually within a few years, and the fix is a proper
membrane rather than more shingles.

**Layer count.** We check the eaves and rake edges for how many layers are up there before
quoting a tear-off. It changes labour and disposal significantly.

**Decking type.** Plank decking with gaps between boards is common on the oldest properties
and needs a different fastening approach than plywood.

**Chimneys.** A high proportion of homes in this age band have masonry chimneys, and
chimney flashing is the most-failed detail on any roof. Original counter flashing cut into
deteriorating mortar is a slow leak waiting for a wet spring.

## Timing work around the university calendar

If a property is student-occupied, scheduling matters more than usual. Roofing is loud and
runs through the working day. For occupied rentals, the practical windows are summer, the
gap between spring and autumn tenancies, or an agreed date with tenants who are aware it's
happening. Emergency work obviously doesn't wait, but planned replacements almost always go
better when they're scheduled rather than squeezed in.
