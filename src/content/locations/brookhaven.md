## A neighbourhood on its second and third roofs

Brookhaven's sections stretch from West Main Street up to Rock Creek Road, broadly between
36th and 48th, with Brookhaven Village anchoring the corner of West Robinson and 36th Avenue
NW. Development began in the late 1960s and continued for decades, which is why the average
home here dates to the mid-1980s and why the neighbourhood reads as established rather than
uniform.

For roofing, the age profile is the whole story. A home built in 1985 has almost certainly
been re-roofed at least once, quite possibly twice. The current covering, not the house, is
what determines where you stand, and a surprising number of homeowners here don't know when
their current roof went on, particularly if they bought the house after it was done.

That's worth resolving before it resolves itself. A roof at year 19 and a roof at year 12
look nearly identical from the street and are in completely different financial positions.

## What we find most often on Brookhaven roofs

**Granule loss on south and west slopes.** Sun exposure is the main non-storm ageing factor
in Oklahoma, and it is not even across a roof. The south and west planes typically fail
first, sometimes by years. A roof can be genuinely finished on one side and sound on the other.

**Shaded slopes with algae streaking and moss.** The flip side of mature trees. The dark
streaking is cosmetic; persistent moss holding moisture against the shingle is not.

**Blocked valleys.** Where leaf litter packs into a valley and stays wet, the shingles
underneath deteriorate visibly faster than the surrounding field.

**Undersized or sagging gutters.** Thirty-year-old gutter runs under heavy tree cover, cleaned
irregularly, holding standing water. Sagging runs and overflow at the front edge are common
here and are usually fixable without full replacement. See
[gutter installation and repair](/services/gutter-installation-repair/).

**Older layovers.** Second layers applied when that was more accepted, hiding decking nobody
has looked at since the 1990s.

## Planning rather than reacting

The most useful thing a Brookhaven homeowner can do is find out how many years the current
roof has left, and put a number in the budget for the year it runs out. Roofs replaced on a
plan get competitive quotes, an unhurried material choice and a scheduled date. Roofs
replaced in an emergency, or in the fortnight after a regional hail event when every roofer
in Cleveland County is booked, get whatever is available.

A [free roof inspection](/services/roof-inspection/) gives you the number and a photo report
to keep.
