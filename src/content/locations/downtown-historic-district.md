## Why these roofs need a different approach

A 1920s Craftsman bungalow and a 1998 subdivision house are the same product only in the
sense that both keep rain out. Everything that determines how the work is done is different.

**The roof is part of a protected streetscape.** In a zoned historic district the roof is
one of the most visually dominant elements of a building, and it is treated accordingly.
Material, profile and colour can all be within the scope of review. This is not an obstacle
so much as a step to plan for; it just needs to happen before the materials are ordered
rather than after the neighbours have complained.

**The structure is a century old.** Rafters were sized to the conventions of the day and
sheathed with boards. Original nail patterns, previous re-roofs and a hundred years of
Oklahoma temperature cycling all leave the deck in a condition that has to be assessed
rather than assumed.

**The details are the job.** On a simple modern roof, the field is most of the work. On
these houses the field is the easy part and the time goes into valleys, dormer cheeks, porch
tie-ins, chimney reglets and eave details that are visible from the pavement and have to
look right as well as work.

## Materials worth considering here

**Dimensional shingles in a historic-appropriate profile.** Several manufacturers produce
lines specifically designed to read as wood shake or slate. These are usually the path of
least resistance for review and generally suit the architecture better than a modern
high-contrast blend.

**Standing seam or stamped metal**, on houses where a metal roof is historically defensible
for that building type. Long-lived and often welcomed, but it needs to be right for the
specific house rather than a general preference.

**Genuine slate or cedar**, where the original material was slate or shake and the budget
allows. Rare in Norman and a specialist job, but occasionally the correct answer.

What we would steer you away from is choosing a product from a sample chip without
considering how it reads across a whole steep roof plane at street distance, and without
checking whether it needs review first.

## Working on an occupied historic home

These are frequently owner-occupied family homes rather than rentals, and the work is more
intrusive because there is more of it. What we control: staging that protects mature
landscaping and original masonry, dust and debris containment, daily magnetic sweeps of the
yard and pavement, and no roof left open overnight unless it's dried in.
