## South of the river, and growing

Goldsby occupies a particular niche in the Norman-area housing market: close enough to Norman
and Oklahoma City to commute, far enough out to buy land, and across the county line in
McClain County. The Interstate 35 corridor makes the commute straightforward, and the
Canadian River makes the boundary feel like a real separation rather than a line on a map.

The consequence for roofing is a housing profile unlike the older communities around Norman.
Where Lexington and Noble have age, Goldsby has scale and complexity. These are largely modern
homes, frequently large, often architecturally busy, on open ground.

## Complexity is what costs money

Homeowners reasonably assume a roof is priced by area. It's priced by area and complexity,
and on the newer large homes around Goldsby, complexity often dominates.

The open field of a roof goes down fast. What takes time is everything else: cutting and
lining valleys, detailing hips and ridges, flashing dormer cheeks and sidewalls, working
around bay projections, tying garage wings into the main structure. A roof with six
intersecting planes has several times the linear footage of hand-worked detail that a simple
gable of the same area has.

That's why a 3,000 square foot roof on a cut-up two-storey can cost more than a 3,500 square
foot roof on a straightforward ranch. It's also why a bid that prices a complex roof like a
simple one is a bid that will need to cut something later to make the number work, and what
gets cut is almost always flashing.

## Wind on open ground

Goldsby's newer developments sit on what was recently farmland. Landscaping is immature,
there's little to break the wind, and many of the houses are tall.

The damage pattern that follows is consistent:

**Ridge caps first.** The most exposed component on any roof, and the first to lift or go
missing.

**Rake and eave edges.** Where wind gets under the covering. Damage concentrates here and
works inward.

**Broken seal strips across the field.** The invisible one. Each shingle bonds to the course
below through a heat-activated adhesive strip; high wind breaks that bond, and the shingle
settles back looking untouched while being effectively loose. It will lift again in the next
storm and let water underneath.

**Creasing.** A horizontal fold line across a shingle means it was folded back and the
fibreglass mat is fractured along that line, whether or not the shingle went anywhere.

None of that is visible from the driveway, which is why wind damage in newer developments so
often goes unclaimed while hail damage on the same roof gets addressed.

## Making the first replacement count

For most Goldsby homes, the first re-roof is the chance to correct the builder's
specification: full tear-off so the decking is actually inspected, all flashing replaced
rather than reused, intake ventilation balanced against the ridge vent that was installed,
and a shingle chosen for this climate. Class 4 impact-resistant products are worth pricing
here, so ask your carrier what premium discount applies, because that number changes the maths
and it's specific to your policy.
