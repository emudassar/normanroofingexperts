## Why does roof leak repair matter in Severn?

Severn lies north-west of Millersville, between Severna Park and the BWI and Fort Meade side of the county. Much of its housing is newer than in the waterfront communities to the east, and a large share of it is townhomes. That shapes both how roofs leak and how repairs get organised.

On attached homes, roofs run continuously across several units. The line between two homes (often over a firewall or party wall) is a transition that has to be sealed carefully, and water entering there can show up in either home. A leak in your upstairs ceiling might start on your section, your neighbour's or right on the joint. Finding out which is the first and most important step, because it decides who needs to be involved.

Newer single-family homes in Severn tend to leak first at small details: cracked rubber pipe boots, exhaust caps, skylight flashing and the occasional exposed nail. These are typically quick repairs if caught early, before water soaks the insulation. Wherever you are, the general advice in [the main Severna Park leak repair page](/) applies: find the real source before anyone starts patching.

Townhome roofs also tend to be simpler in shape but busier in detail. Rows of identical vents, stepped roof lines where units sit at different heights, and dormers close together all mean more joints per square foot than on a plain single-family roof. Each joint is a place where flashing has to be right.

## Which leak repairs do Severn homes need most?

### Leak detection on attached homes

With shared roofs and party walls, guessing is expensive. A methodical trace, sometimes with a water test, shows exactly where water enters. [Roof leak detection](/roof-leak-detection/) explains how it's done.

### Pipe boot and vent repair

Cracked boots and loose vents are among the most common leaks on newer roofs. See [roof vent leak repair](/roof-vent-leak-repair/).

Skylights in upstairs bathrooms and stairwells can leak at the flashing or suffer condensation in humid weather. [Skylight leak repair](/skylight-leak-repair/) covers how to tell the two apart.

## Severn roof leak checklist

- Check your HOA documents to see who is responsible for the roof.
- If you're in a townhome, ask your neighbours whether they've seen stains too.
- Look at pipe boots and vent caps from the ground every year or so.
- Note whether bathroom drips happen in rain or only after showers.
- Photograph stains and keep dates for any HOA or neighbour conversation.
- Keep gutters clear, especially on shared runs.

## What should Severn homeowners ask the roofer?

If you live in a townhome, the questions are as much about coordination as about roofing.

- **Is the leak on my section, my neighbour's, or the shared joint?** Ask for photos that show where the entry point is relative to the property line, so you can share them with a neighbour or your HOA.
- **Can my section be repaired on its own?** Most repairs can. If the repair has to cross the line, ask the roofer how they'd tie into the neighbouring roof and whether they need permission first.
- **Does the shingle match what the rest of the row has?** On a continuous roof, a patch in a different color stands out. Ask about matching before work begins, especially if your HOA has appearance rules.
- **Are the vents on my section the same age as the shingles?** On newer roofs, boots are often the first part to fail, and checking them all at once avoids another visit.

For single-family homes, the usual questions apply: what failed, why, and what was done, with photos. If you rent rather than own, let your landlord or property manager know about the leak before arranging a repair, since roof work is usually the owner's decision.

Townhome rows also share gutters and downspouts, so an overflowing gutter on one unit can push water into the next. If your neighbour's gutter is clogged, it may be part of your leak.
