## What is skylight leak repair?

Skylight leak repair stops water from entering through or around a roof window. A skylight is a deliberate hole in the roof, framed with a curb or deck-mounted frame, sealed to the glass, and tied into the shingles with a flashing kit. Water can get past any of those layers: the flashing, the gaskets and seals around the glass, the frame itself, or the shingles around it.

The repair might be as small as re-sealing a corner or as large as installing a new unit with fresh flashing. What makes skylights tricky is that a very common "leak" isn't a leak at all. It's condensation. Telling the two apart is the first step, and it's part of why we put together [our Severna Park leak repair guide](/) to help homeowners describe what they're seeing.

## How does skylight leak repair work?

**Diagnosis comes first.** The roofer looks at where and when water appears. Drips during rain at the upper corners suggest flashing. Water along the bottom edge can be flashing, or debris damming water against the frame. Fog between glass panes means the insulated glass seal has failed. Water beading all over the inside glass on a cold, dry morning is condensation.

**Flashing repair or replacement.** Most skylights use a step-flashing kit around the sides, a head flashing at the top and a sill flashing at the bottom. If the kit is damaged or was installed incorrectly, the roofer lifts the shingles, removes it and installs new flashing, ideally the manufacturer's kit made for that model. See [roof flashing repair](/roof-flashing-repair/) for how these layers are woven together.

**Seal and frame repair.** Some units allow gaskets or glazing seals to be replaced. Cracked plastic domes or broken frames usually mean replacement.

**Condensation fixes.** If the problem is moisture from inside the house, the work is ventilation: running bathroom fans, improving airflow, or adding insulation to the skylight shaft.

## What are the warning signs of a skylight leak?

<ul class="sign-list">
<li>Drips from the corners or edges of the frame during rain.</li>
<li>Brown or yellow stains on the drywall around the skylight shaft.</li>
<li>Peeling paint or swollen trim around the opening.</li>
<li>Fog or moisture trapped between the glass panes.</li>
<li>Mold or mildew spots on the shaft walls.</li>
<li>Leaves and debris piled at the uphill side of the skylight.</li>
<li>Cracked plastic dome or visible gaps in the exterior frame.</li>
<li>A stain that grows after each storm rather than staying the same.</li>
</ul>

## What causes skylights to leak?

The most common cause is **flashing**. A skylight installed with sealant instead of a proper kit, or with old flashing reused when the roof was redone, is a leak waiting to happen. **Age** is next: gaskets and seals harden over the years and stop sealing, and plastic domes yellow and crack.

**Debris** matters more than people expect. In leafy neighborhoods, leaves and needles collect against the upper edge of the skylight, hold water there, and let it creep under the head flashing. **Ice** can do the same in winter. Finally, **condensation** comes from warm, humid indoor air meeting cold glass. It's especially common in bathrooms and kitchens and during the humid Maryland summer when air conditioning chills the glass from inside.

## What can you safely check yourself first?

Check the weather pattern first. Did the water show up in rain, or on a cold, dry morning? Look at the glass: beads all over the inside surface are condensation, while fog between the panes is a failed seal. Look closely at where the water marks start (corner, top edge, bottom edge).

From the ground, see if leaves are piled behind the skylight. In the attic, if the skylight shaft is accessible, check for wet framing and insulation around it.

<aside class="callout callout--tip"><p><strong>Pro tip:</strong> Tape a dry paper towel along the bottom inside edge of the frame before the next rain. If it's wet afterwards and the glass isn't fogged, the water is coming from outside. If the glass is beaded and the towel is damp too, think condensation.</p></aside>

## What happens when the roofer visits?

1. **Inside review.** The roofer checks the stain pattern, the glass and the shaft for signs of condensation or seal failure.
2. **Roof inspection.** The flashing kit, shingles around the unit, frame and seals are examined, and debris is cleared.
3. **Water test if unclear.** Water is run over one side at a time to find the entry point.
4. **Recommendation.** Repair (re-seal or re-flash) or replace, with the reasons for each option.
5. **Repair.** New flashing is woven into the shingles, seals are replaced or a new unit is installed, and the interior is checked after a test.

## How do you keep a skylight from leaking?

Most skylight leaks are preventable with simple habits, and a well-installed unit can stay dry for many years.

**Keep the uphill side clear.** In leafy neighborhoods, debris collects above the skylight every fall. Have it cleared along with the gutters, especially if you can see leaves piling up from the ground.

**Control indoor humidity.** In bathrooms and kitchens, run the exhaust fan during and after showers and cooking. Lower humidity means less condensation on the glass and fewer drips that look like leaks.

**Look at the inside frame each season.** A quick check for new stains, peeling paint or swollen trim catches a problem early. Fog between the panes is a sign the glass seal is going and the unit may be near the end of its life.

**Use the right flashing kit.** If a skylight is ever replaced or re-flashed, ask for the manufacturer's flashing kit made for that model. It's designed to work with the frame and shingles, which sealant alone can't do.

**Plan around roof work.** If you're replacing the roof and the skylight is old, consider replacing the skylight at the same time. It avoids cutting into new shingles later, and the flashing should be new either way.

## When is a skylight leak an emergency?

A leak becomes urgent when water is pouring in rather than dripping, when a dome or glass panel is cracked or broken, or when a falling branch has hit the skylight. Broken glass also creates a safety risk below.
