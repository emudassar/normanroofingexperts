## What is chimney leak repair?

Chimney leak repair is the work that stops rain from getting into the house through or around a chimney. A chimney is the biggest hole in most roofs, and it's made of materials (brick, mortar, stone, metal and sealant) that all move and age differently. That makes it one of the most common leak points on older homes, and on many of the brick-chimney houses built across Anne Arundel County in the second half of the last century.

The repair itself might be new counter flashing, a rebuilt crown, repointed mortar, a new cap, or a cricket on the uphill side. Getting the diagnosis right is the whole job, because a chimney can leak through any of these. ## How does chimney leak repair work?

A good repair starts by working out which of the five water paths is open.

**Flashing.** Base or step flashing sits under the shingles against the chimney sides, and counter flashing is cut into the mortar to cover it. When the counter flashing pulls out of the joint or its sealant fails, water runs behind it. The fix is resetting or replacing the counter flashing, and often the step flashing below it. Our [roof flashing repair](/roof-flashing-repair/) page explains these layers in detail.

**Crown.** The crown is the concrete or mortar slab at the top of the chimney. Cracks let water straight into the masonry. Small cracks can be sealed with a crown coating; a crumbling crown needs rebuilding.

**Masonry.** Brick and especially old mortar absorb water. Missing or cracked joints are cut out and repointed with fresh mortar. Once the joints are sound, a breathable water repellent can reduce absorption.

**Cap.** An open flue lets rain fall straight down the chimney. A cap with a mesh screen stops rain and animals.

**Cricket.** On a wide chimney, water and leaves pile up behind it. A cricket diverts that water to each side.

## What are the warning signs of a chimney leak?

<ul class="sign-list">
<li>Stains on the ceiling or wall right beside the chimney or fireplace.</li>
<li>Water or rust in the firebox, or a rusted damper.</li>
<li>White, chalky deposits on brick, inside or outside.</li>
<li>Cracked or crumbling mortar joints visible from the ground.</li>
<li>Counter flashing pulled out of the mortar line.</li>
<li>A musty or damp smell from the fireplace after rain.</li>
<li>Leaves and debris piled behind the chimney on the roof.</li>
<li>Peeling paint or bubbling plaster on the chimney breast inside.</li>
</ul>

## What causes chimneys to leak?

Masonry is porous by nature, so time alone causes much of it. Every freeze-thaw cycle drives water into small cracks, freezes, expands and widens them. Maryland winters bring plenty of nights that dip below freezing followed by milder days, which makes this cycle a steady source of damage to crowns and mortar.

Flashing is the other big cause. A chimney and the roof framing move independently, so the joint between them is under constant stress, and the sealant that seals counter flashing dries out in summer sun. Missing caps, crowns built with plain mortar instead of a proper crown mix, and wide chimneys without crickets round out the list. In wooded neighborhoods, leaves piling up behind the chimney hold water against the flashing for days after each storm.

## What can you safely check yourself first?

From the ground with binoculars, look at the top of the chimney: is there a cap? Is the crown cracked? Are mortar joints missing? Look at where the metal meets the brick for gaps.

Inside, open the damper with a flashlight on a dry day and look for rust or water marks. In the attic, look at the framing around the chimney for dark stains, and compare the damp spots to the chimney sides. Water on the uphill side suggests the cricket area; water on all sides points to the crown or masonry.

<aside class="callout callout--tip"><p><strong>Pro tip:</strong> Note whether the leak shows up during every rain or only during long, heavy storms. Leaks only in long storms often come from water soaking through masonry, while every-rain leaks point more to flashing.</p></aside>

## What happens when the roofer visits?

1. **Inside first.** The roofer looks at the stain, the attic framing and the firebox to see where the water appears.
2. **Roof and chimney inspection.** Flashing, crown, cap, mortar and the area behind the chimney are checked.
3. **Water test if needed.** Running water on one part at a time, starting low and working up, isolates the source.
4. **Repair plan.** You get a clear explanation of which parts are leaking and which work is roofing versus masonry.
5. **Repair and photos.** Flashing is reset or replaced, masonry repairs are done, and photos show the finished details.

## How do you keep a chimney from leaking again?

A chimney repair lasts longest when the whole system is treated, not just the spot that failed. Once the leak is fixed, a few habits keep it that way.

**Keep the top covered.** A properly sized cap with mesh keeps rain, leaves and animals out of the flue. If the crown was sealed or rebuilt, ask how often the provider suggests checking the coating; crowns take the most direct weather of any part of the house.

**Watch the mortar.** Mortar is meant to be softer than brick and to wear first. When joints start to recess or crumble, repointing early is far cheaper than replacing spalled bricks later. Look at the chimney from the ground each spring, after the freeze-thaw season has done its work.

**Clear the uphill side.** On wooded lots, leaves and twigs collect behind the chimney every fall. If your chimney has no cricket and debris piles up there, ask whether adding one makes sense the next time the roof is worked on.

**Don't seal what should breathe.** Paints and non-breathable sealers can trap moisture inside brick, which then freezes and flakes the face off. If a water repellent is used, it should be one made for masonry that lets vapor escape.

**Pair it with roof work.** When the roof is replaced, the chimney flashing should be replaced too, never bent back and reused. Mention this to anyone quoting new shingles.

## When is a chimney leak an emergency?

It's urgent when water is running into the firebox or down the wall during a storm, when the chimney has visibly shifted or lost bricks, or when a storm tore the flashing loose. Those can let in a lot of water fast or signal a structural problem.

<aside class="callout callout--warn"><p><strong>Warning:</strong> Don't light a fire if water has been getting into the chimney until it has been inspected. Moisture can damage the flue liner, and a damaged liner is a fire risk.</p></aside>
