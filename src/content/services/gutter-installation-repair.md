## Why gutters matter more in central Oklahoma than most places

The soil under Norman is largely expansive clay. It absorbs water and swells; it dries and
shrinks. Foundations sit on top of that movement, and the more extreme the wet-dry cycle
immediately around the house, the more the foundation moves.

Uncontrolled roof runoff makes that cycle as extreme as it can be: an enormous volume of
water concentrated in a narrow strip right at the foundation, several times a season,
followed by a bone-dry August. Gutters that carry water ten feet from the house cost a
fraction of what foundation repair does, and this is the actual argument for them, not the
appearance of the fascia board.

The second reason is that Oklahoma rain arrives fast. Rainfall intensity, not annual total,
determines whether a gutter copes. A system perfectly adequate for steady rain will overflow
in a storm that drops an inch in twenty minutes, which here is an ordinary spring afternoon.

## How gutters usually fail

**Undersized for the roof.** The most common problem and the least visible one, because it
only shows up in heavy rain, by which point nobody's outside watching.

**Lost pitch.** Gutters need consistent fall to the outlets. Hangers loosen, fascia softens,
and a run goes flat or reverses. Standing water is heavy, roughly eight pounds per gallon,
and that weight accelerates the sag until the run tears off.

**Too few downspouts.** Long runs with a single outlet back up at the far end regardless of
gutter size. Adding an outlet is often the cheapest fix available for an overflow problem.

**Seam failure.** Sectional gutter leaks at joints as sealant ages and metal expands and
contracts through Oklahoma's temperature swings. Seamless construction eliminates most of
these by design.

**Discharge too close to the house.** Water leaving the downspout eighteen inches from the
foundation has not been controlled, it's been relocated by eighteen inches.

**Fascia rot.** Usually caused by missing or badly installed drip edge letting water wick
behind the gutter. It progresses invisibly until a gutter comes away in your hand.

## Timing gutter work with roof work

If you are replacing your roof, that is the moment to sort the gutters. Drip edge and
starter courses are installed at the same edge the gutter hangs from, access equipment is
already on site, and any fascia repair is far easier with the roof edge open. Doing them
together costs less than doing them a year apart, and it avoids new gutter being damaged
during a tear-off.
