## What actually determines how long your new roof lasts

Homeowners are usually shown a shingle brand and a warranty length. Neither is a reliable
predictor. In practice, roofs in Cleveland County fail early for four reasons, and only one
of them is the shingle.

**Decking.** New shingles nailed to soft sheathing will not hold. Fasteners lose grip,
shingles lift in wind, and the failure often shows up within a few seasons. A bid that does
not state a decking replacement rate is a bid that intends to discover the problem after
you've signed.

**Fastening.** Shingles have a nailing zone. Nails placed high, above the zone, hold only
one layer instead of two, which is fine on a still day and disastrous in a 60 mph gust. This
is invisible from the ground the moment the next course is laid, which is precisely why it
happens.

**Ventilation.** An attic without balanced intake and exhaust runs far hotter than the
outside air in an Oklahoma July. That heat degrades the asphalt from underneath. Roofs in
poorly ventilated attics routinely fail years short of rating, and it is a warranty exclusion
for most manufacturers.

**Flashing.** Almost every leak on an otherwise healthy roof traces to a flashing detail. New
shingles with old flashing is a new roof with the old roof's weak points intact.

## Choosing materials for this climate

**Architectural asphalt shingles** are the default for good reason: they handle Oklahoma
temperature swings well, they're economical to repair, and every roofer in the state can
service them.

**Class 4 impact-resistant shingles** are the upgrade worth considering here. Worth asking
your insurer what discount they offer before you decide, because that number changes the maths
considerably and it is specific to your carrier.

**Metal**, standing seam or stone-coated steel, lasts far longer, sheds hail better in
some profiles, and costs substantially more. It can also dent visibly under large hail
without losing function, which some homeowners find unacceptable and others don't mind at
all. Worth a conversation rather than a brochure.

## How to compare three roofing bids properly

Ask every contractor, in writing:

- Is this a full tear-off, and does the price include disposal?
- What is your per-sheet rate for replacing decking, and what triggers replacement?
- Is all flashing being replaced, or reused?
- What underlayment, and where does ice-and-water membrane go?
- Is ventilation being modified, and to what specification?
- What exactly does your workmanship warranty cover, and what voids it?

Bids that look wildly different on price usually differ on these answers, not on the
shingle. If you'd like an assessment before you commit to anything, book a free
[roof inspection](/services/roof-inspection/).
